## Design Context

### Users
Bible enthusiasts spanning two main contexts: solo casual players who enjoy quiet scripture knowledge challenges in their own time, and group/multiplayer settings like Bible study groups or church gatherings where the competitive element is central. Players range from deeply knowledgeable scripture scholars to curious newcomers — both demographics play simultaneously in multiplayer rooms.

### Brand Personality
**Playful, nerdy, warm.** Not reverent or solemn, but not frivolous either. The game celebrates Bible knowledge with enthusiasm and good humor. Think: the friend who loves Bible trivia, gets excited when someone nails an obscure verse, and makes everyone feel welcome regardless of how much they know.

### Aesthetic Direction
Dark premium with warm gold accents (`--gold: #c9a644`). Deep brown-black backgrounds (`#0d0b09`) that evoke evening reading by candlelight rather than cold corporate dark mode. Gold as a reward color — it appears on success, interaction, and emphasis. The warmth is baked into the palette; animations should reinforce that warmth with smooth, organic motion rather than sharp or mechanical transitions.

**Anti-references**: Avoid anything that feels like a cold tech product, a church slideshow, or a mobile casino. The goal is "beloved indie game" not "corporate Bible app."

**Theme**: Dark only — the deep warm dark is central to the aesthetic identity.

### Design Principles
1. **Warmth through motion** — Animations should feel like pages turning, not screens flipping. Smooth deceleration, no bounce/elastic. The easing should feel organic.
2. **Reward the guess moment** — The score reveal is the emotional peak of every round. It deserves ceremony without being slow or blocking.
3. **Playful but not silly** — Micro-interactions can have personality (a subtle spring on a correct answer), but should never feel gimmicky or random.
4. **Group dynamics matter** — Multiplayer moments (players joining, round transitions, final standings) should feel social and celebratory — like a game show, not a spreadsheet.
5. **Accessibility is non-negotiable** — `prefers-reduced-motion` must be respected throughout; all animations have functional non-animated fallbacks.
